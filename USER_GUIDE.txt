1. THE BIG PICTURE
================================================================================

  WHAT IS MAMMOGRAPHY?
  A mammogram is an X-ray image of a woman's breast. Doctors use mammograms
  to look for signs of breast cancer - before the patient even has symptoms.
  This is called SCREENING.

  WHAT ARE WE LOOKING FOR?
  Two types of suspicious findings:

    MASS        - A solid lump of tissue. Could be benign (harmless)
                  or malignant (cancer).

    CALCIFICATION (CALC) - Tiny calcium deposits. Individually harmless,
                  but clusters of them can be an early sign of cancer.

  Each of these can be:
    Benign    = NOT cancer (still needs monitoring)
    Malignant = CANCER (needs immediate treatment)

  So we have 4 CLASSES in total:

  +----+------------------+-------------------------------+
  | ID | Class Name       | Meaning                       |
  +----+------------------+-------------------------------+
  |  0 | benign_mass      | Harmless lump                 |
  |  1 | malignant_mass   | Cancerous lump                |
  |  2 | benign_calc      | Harmless calcium cluster      |
  |  3 | malignant_calc   | Cancerous calcium cluster     |
  +----+------------------+-------------------------------+

  WHAT DOES OUR AI DO?
  Our AI looks at a mammogram image and draws BOXES around suspicious areas,
  then labels each box with one of the 4 classes above. This is called
  OBJECT DETECTION.

  WHAT IS THE CORE IDEA OF OUR PAPER?
  We ask: "Is ONE AI better than MANY specialized AIs?"

  Option A - UNIVERSAL MODEL:
    One single AI trained to detect all 4 types at once.

  Option B - MOE (MIXTURE OF EXPERTS):
    Two specialized AIs:
      - Mass Expert  : only looks for masses (benign and malignant)
      - Calc Expert  : only looks for calcifications (benign and malignant)
    We combine their predictions = Mixture of Experts (MoE)

  OUR CLAIM:
  The specialized MoE is BETTER than the single Universal model on CBIS-DDSM
  mammography data.

2. BASIC CONCEPTS - EXPLAINED SIMPLY
================================================================================

  2.1  WHAT IS A BOUNDING BOX?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  A bounding box is a rectangle drawn around an object in an image.
  It has 4 numbers: x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max (top-left & bottom-right).
  When a radiologist annotates a mammogram, they draw a box around the
  suspicious area. Our AI must predict these boxes.

  2.2  WHAT IS IOU (INTERSECTION OVER UNION)?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  When our AI predicts a box, we need to check:
  "How close is this prediction to the real (ground truth) box?"

  IoU = Area of Overlap / Area of Union

    IoU = 1.0  -->  Perfect match (boxes are identical)
    IoU = 0.5  -->  Boxes overlap by 50%
    IoU = 0.0  -->  No overlap at all

  We use IoU >= 0.30 for FROC evaluation (lenient - mammography is hard).
  We use IoU >= 0.50 for mAP evaluation (standard computer vision).

  2.3  WHAT IS mAP50?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  mAP = Mean Average Precision. Most common metric in object detection.

  PRECISION : Of all boxes our AI drew, what fraction were correct?
              (Low precision = AI draws too many wrong boxes)

  RECALL    : Of all real boxes (ground truth), what fraction did AI find?
              (Low recall = AI misses many real lesions)

  These have a trade-off. Average Precision (AP) = area under the
  Precision-Recall curve. One number summarizing the whole trade-off.

  mAP    = Mean AP across all classes.
  mAP50  = mAP at IoU threshold = 0.50.
           A prediction counts correct only if it overlaps GT by >= 50%.
  mAP50-95 = Average of mAP at IoU thresholds 0.50 to 0.95 (very strict).

  IN PLAIN ENGLISH:
  mAP50 = 0.50 means AI correctly finds 50% of lesions with correct boxes.
  Our numbers (~0.06) sound low, but mammography is extremely hard.
  Lesions are tiny, rare, and look like normal tissue.

  2.4  WHAT IS FROC?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  FROC = Free-Response Receiver Operating Characteristic.
  It is the GOLD STANDARD metric in medical imaging / mammography.

  Regular metrics assume every image has an object. FROC handles the
  real world: most mammograms are NORMAL (no cancer). A radiologist
  looking at 100 mammograms might find only 3-4 with cancer.

  FROC plots:
    X-axis : Average False Positives per image (FP/image).
             How many wrong "cancer" alarms per scan?
    Y-axis : Sensitivity. How many real cancers did we catch?

  SENSITIVITY AT 2 FP/IMAGE (S@2):
  At the point where our AI triggers 2 false alarms per image,
  what fraction of real cancers does it catch?
  This is our PRIMARY FROC metric.

  WHY 2 FP/IMAGE?
  Clinically relevant - a radiologist can handle ~2 flagged areas per scan.
  More than that = too many false alarms.

  IN PLAIN ENGLISH:
  S@2 = 0.83 means: at 2 false alarms per scan, our AI catches 83%
  of real cancers. That is our INbreast external validation result.

  2.5  WHAT IS YOLO?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  YOLO = You Only Look Once. A family of deep learning models for
  real-time object detection.

  How it works (simplified):
    1. Divides the image into a grid
    2. Each grid cell predicts boxes and class labels
    3. Does this in ONE pass through the neural network ("once")
    4. Very fast (real-time capable)

  We use YOLO11l (YOLO version 11, large variant).
  "Large" = more parameters, slower but more accurate.

  Key settings:
    imgsz=1280   -->  Image resized to 1280x1280 for training
    epochs=100+  -->  100-150 passes through the full dataset
    nc=2         -->  Expert models (2 classes each)
    nc=4         -->  Universal model (4 classes)

  2.6  WHAT IS A MIXTURE OF EXPERTS (MOE)?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  MoE = design pattern where multiple specialized models ("experts")
  each handle a part of the problem, and their outputs are combined.

  DOMAIN-SPLIT MOE (our main method - simpler):
    - Mass expert handles mass classes (0, 1)
    - Calc expert handles calc classes (2, 3)
    - We run BOTH on every image and merge their predictions
    - No router needed - each expert covers its own class domain

  GATED MOE (our ablation):
    - Uses a router classifier to look at the image
    - Router gives confidence score (e.g., "80% mass, 20% calc")
    - Score scales expert predictions
    - Result: WORSE than no routing (router only 71% accurate)

  WHY DOES MOE MAKE SENSE FOR MAMMOGRAPHY?
  Mass lesions and calcifications look completely different:
    Mass  = large, smooth-edged soft tissue density
    Calc  = tiny bright specks (1-2mm), often in clusters
  A single model must learn two very different visual patterns.
  Dedicated experts can focus completely.

  2.7  WHAT IS TRAIN / VAL / TEST SPLIT?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  When training AI, we split data into 3 sets:

  +---------------+----------------------------------+----------------------+
  | Set           | Purpose                          | Used when            |
  +---------------+----------------------------------+----------------------+
  | Train         | Model learns from this           | During training      |
  | Val (Valid.)  | Tune hyperparams, pick best ep.  | During training      |
  | Test          | Final honest evaluation          | Only ONCE at the end |
  +---------------+----------------------------------+----------------------+

  The TEST SET must never influence training or model selection.
  If you peek at test results to adjust your model = DATA LEAKAGE.

  2.8  WHAT IS PATIENT-LEVEL SPLIT?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  In medical imaging, one patient can have multiple images.
  If Patient A's images go into both train AND test, the model memorizes
  Patient A and reports falsely high accuracy.

  We use PATIENT-LEVEL GroupShuffleSplit:
  All images from one patient are ONLY in one split (train OR val OR test).
  This is the correct, leakage-free approach.

  2.9  WHAT IS A SEED?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  A random seed is a number controlling randomness in code.
  Same seed = same random split every time.
  Different seed = different split.

  We use 3 seeds (42, 1, 123) to create 3 different splits,
  train 3 models, and report mean +/- std. This shows our results
  are not a fluke of one lucky split.

  2.10  WHAT IS MEAN +/- STD?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  mean = average of 3 values
  std  = standard deviation = how spread out the values are

  Example: seeds give mAP50 = 0.05, 0.06, 0.08
    mean = 0.063, std = 0.015  -->  written as  0.063 +/- 0.015

  Small std = consistent results across seeds.

4. THE PROBLEM WE DISCOVERED - DATA LEAKAGE
================================================================================

  WHAT HAPPENED?
  Early in this project (before the canonical rebuild), we had results:
    Calc mAP50 = 0.4024
    Mass mAP50 = 0.447
  These looked great. But they were WRONG.

  WHY WERE THEY WRONG?
  Images were split randomly at the IMAGE LEVEL, not the PATIENT LEVEL.
  One patient might have 10 mammogram images.
  Some went into train, some into test.
  The model memorized patient-specific tissue patterns during training,
  then "recognized" the same patient in the test set.

  This is like studying past exam papers - you memorize answers, not
  the subject. The AI seemed smart but was just memorizing.

  HOW MUCH INFLATION?
  About 75% of performance was artificial.

  +---------------+--------------------+--------------------+
  | Model         | Inflated (leaky)   | Real (leakage-free)|
  +---------------+--------------------+--------------------+
  | Calc mAP50    |      0.4024        |   0.0876 (true)    |
  | Mass mAP50    |      0.447         |   0.434  (holds)   |
  +---------------+--------------------+--------------------+

  Calc COLLAPSED to near zero. Mass held up (masses are genuinely learnable).

  HOW WE FIXED IT:
  GroupShuffleSplit by PATIENT ID. All images from one patient go to
  exactly one split. Same seed=42 used across ALL models so comparisons
  are fair.

  WHY OUR NUMBERS LOOK "LOW":
  They are HONEST. Low honest numbers are publishable.
  High inflated numbers get papers rejected or retracted.

5. EVERY EXPERIMENT WE RAN
================================================================================

  PHASE 1: EARLY EXPERIMENTS ON KAGGLE T4 GPU - WITH LEAKAGE [HISTORICAL]
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  These were exploratory. Results were inflated. NOT used in paper.

  Exp 1-10 : Testing YOLO versions, image sizes, hyperparameters.
             Image-level random split (leaky).
             Numbers: mass ~0.45, calc ~0.40 (both inflated, WRONG).

  Exp 11-15: Testing CLAHE (contrast enhancement), mosaic augmentation,
             different batch sizes. Key findings:
               - CLAHE HURTS calc detection (proven, do not use)
               - Mosaic augmentation hurts calc on clean split
               - batch=1 collapses BatchNorm (must keep batch >= 4)

  PHASE 2: LEAKAGE DISCOVERY AND FIX [CRITICAL TURNING POINT]
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Noticed numbers were too good to be true. Investigated.
  Found the image-level split was the culprit.
  Rebuilt everything from scratch with patient-level GroupShuffleSplit.

  PHASE 3: CANONICAL REBUILD - FROM SCRATCH [THE FOUNDATION]
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

  EXP 16 - CALC EXPERT WITH TILING + P2 HEAD
    Problem : Calcs are tiny (1-3mm on a 3000-5000px image).
              YOLO resizes to 1280px, making calcs nearly invisible.
    Attempt : Tile each mammogram into 1024px tiles at native resolution.
              Use YOLOv8l-p2 (stride-4 detection head for tiny objects).
              Use SAHI (Sliced Inference) at test time.
    Result  : Calc mAP50 plateaued at ~0.07. Tiling did NOT fix calc.
    Root cause: 2-class split halves per-class data AND double-penalizes
              the model for confusing benign vs malignant.
              IoU=0.50 is brutal on tiny clusters.
    Conclusion: Calc mAP50 ~0.10 is the genuine ceiling. NOT a training bug.

  EXP 17 - UNIVERSAL BASELINE
    What    : YOLO11l trained on all 4 classes simultaneously.
              Full-image (no tiling). 100 epochs.
    Purpose : The "one model does everything" baseline MoE must beat.
    Dataset : canonical_4class (2190 train / 455 val / 458 test)

  EXP 18 - MASS EXPERT
    What    : YOLO11l trained only on masses (nc=2).
              Full-image. 89-120 epochs.
    Dataset : canonical_mass_clean (604 train / 26 val / 26 test)
    Result  : mAP50 = 0.499 mean (val), 0.434 (test, seed=42) - STRONG!

  PHASE 4: NOTEBOOK 1 - EXPERT TRAINING MULTI-SEED [FINAL]
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Training all experts across 3 seeds (42, 1, 123).

  PHASE 5: NOTEBOOK 3 - MOE EVALUATION MULTI-SEED [FINAL]
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Testing two MoE designs vs Universal on the canonical test set.

  DOMAIN-SPLIT MOE (main method):
    1. Run mass expert on image   --> predictions for classes 0, 1
    2. Run calc expert on image   --> add +2 offset (0->2, 1->3)
    3. Merge both experts' predictions
    4. No router, no confidence scaling

  GATED MOE (ablation):
    1. Run router --> get confidence score
    2. Scale expert predictions by router confidence
    Result: WORSE than no gating (router too inaccurate at 71%)

  FROC only checks if a box HITS ground truth at IoU >= 0.30 (lenient).

  FROC TIE IS A FEATURE, NOT A FAILURE:
  MoE does NOT regress on clinical sensitivity. The mAP improvement
  reflects better localisation quality. The tied FROC means MoE is at
  least as clinically safe as Universal, but with better box placement.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

  Calc Expert outputs :  cls=0 (benign_calc)   cls=1 (malignant_calc)

  benign_mass and benign_calc would both be "class 0" - WRONG!

    Calc cls=0  -->  0 + 2 = cls=2  (benign_calc)
    Calc cls=1  -->  1 + 2 = cls=3  (malignant_calc)

12. TRAINING DETAILS - OPTIMIZER, LOSS, AUGMENTATION (LAYMAN TERMS)
================================================================================

  12.1  WHAT IS AN OPTIMIZER?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Think of training an AI like teaching a student to solve maths problems.

  The student solves a problem, gets it wrong, and the teacher says
  "you are wrong by THIS MUCH - now correct yourself."

  The OPTIMIZER is the rule the student uses to correct themselves.
  We use SGD (Stochastic Gradient Descent).

  HOW SGD WORKS IN 4 STEPS:
    Step 1 : AI looks at a mammogram and predicts bounding boxes (wrong at first)
    Step 2 : Calculate how wrong it is (this is called LOSS - one number)
    Step 3 : SGD says "adjust your internal numbers in this direction"
    Step 4 : Repeat for every image, every epoch

  That is it. SGD just keeps taking small steps toward the correct answer.

  12.2  WHAT IS MOMENTUM? (Value = 0.937)
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Without momentum:
    Imagine walking downhill blindfolded. You stop after every step,
    re-check direction, then take another step. Very slow and jerky.

  With momentum = 0.937:
    Imagine a ball rolling downhill. It builds speed. Even if the ground
    briefly goes uphill slightly, the ball keeps rolling because of speed.

  0.937 means:
    Remember 93.7% of the previous direction
    Add 6.3% of the new direction
    = Smooth, fast movement toward the correct answer

  Think of it like a car with good suspension - small bumps do not
  knock you off course.

  12.3  WHAT IS WEIGHT DECAY? (Value = 0.0005)
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  The AI model has ~25 million numbers inside it called WEIGHTS.
  Sometimes weights grow too large and the model starts memorising
  training images instead of learning patterns. This is called overfitting.

  Weight decay = a tiny penalty every step that pulls weights back toward zero.

  Every training step:
    weight = weight - (0.0005 x weight)

  Think of it like a rubber band attached to every weight.
  It lets weights grow when needed but pulls them back if they get too big.
  Keeps the model from cheating by memorising.

  12.4  WHAT IS LEARNING RATE? (Value = 0.01 start, 0.0001 end)
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Learning rate = HOW BIG A STEP the student takes when correcting.

  +------------------+--------------------------------------------------+
  | Learning Rate    | Effect                                           |
  +------------------+--------------------------------------------------+
  | Too HIGH (0.9)   | Student overcorrects, jumps past the answer,     |
  |                  | never actually learns. Model diverges.           |
  | Too LOW (0.00001)| Student barely moves. Takes forever. Wastes time.|
  | Just right (0.01)| Steady progress toward the correct answer.      |
  +------------------+--------------------------------------------------+

  We start at 0.01 and slowly reduce to 0.0001 by the end of training.

  12.5  WHAT IS COSINE DECAY?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  The learning rate does not stay at 0.01 the whole time. It slowly reduces.

  WHY?
    Early in training  = model knows nothing   = take BIG steps
    Late in training   = model is almost right = take TINY steps (fine-tune)

  COSINE DECAY means the reduction follows a smooth S-curve:

    Epoch 1   --> LR = 0.01    (big steps, learning fast)
    Epoch 50  --> LR = 0.005   (medium steps)
    Epoch 100 --> LR = 0.0001  (tiny steps, fine tuning)

  Think of it like parking a car:
    Drive fast on the road --> slow down as you approach the parking spot
    --> tiny adjustments to park perfectly

  12.6  WHAT IS WARMUP? (3 epochs)
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  At the very start, model weights are random (fresh from ImageNet).
  If you immediately use full learning rate 0.01 on random weights,
  the model panics and makes huge incorrect corrections.

  WARMUP = start with tiny LR for first 3 epochs, then ramp up to 0.01.

    Epoch 1 --> LR = 0.001   (very careful, feeling it out)
    Epoch 2 --> LR = 0.005   (building up)
    Epoch 3 --> LR = 0.009   (almost there)
    Epoch 4 --> LR = 0.01    (full speed from now on)

  Think of it like warming up before a sprint race.
  You do not run at full speed from a dead stop - you build up.

  12.7  WHAT IS LOSS? (How We Measure "How Wrong")
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  LOSS = one single number that measures total mistake of the model.
  Lower loss = better model. The optimizer tries to minimize this number.

  Our model makes 3 types of mistakes, each with a weight:

  +---------------------+-----------------------------+--------+-------------------+
  | Loss Type           | What it penalises           | Weight | Simple meaning    |
  +---------------------+-----------------------------+--------+-------------------+
  | Box loss (CIoU)     | Box in wrong place/size     |   7.5  | Most important    |
  | Class loss (BCE)    | Said benign, was malignant  |   0.5  | Less important    |
  | DFL loss            | Box edges not sharp enough  |   1.5  | Medium            |
  +---------------------+-----------------------------+--------+-------------------+

  Total Loss = (7.5 x box mistake) + (0.5 x class mistake) + (1.5 x edge mistake)

  WHY IS BOX LOSS WEIGHTED 7.5 (HIGHEST)?
  Getting the box in the right place matters most. If you draw a box in
  the completely wrong area of the mammogram, that is the worst mistake.
  Class confusion (benign vs malignant) is also bad but secondary.

  12.8  WHAT IS BATCH SIZE? (Value = 4 minimum)
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Instead of correcting the model after every single image, we show it
  a GROUP of images first, then correct once for the whole group.
  That group size = BATCH SIZE.

  +------------+-----------------------------------------------+
  | Batch Size | Effect                                        |
  +------------+-----------------------------------------------+
  | batch = 1  | Correct after 1 image. Too noisy. Unstable.  |
  |            | Also BREAKS BatchNorm (needs 2+ images)       |
  | batch = 4  | Stable. What we use minimum.                 |
  | batch = 16 | Smoother training but needs more GPU memory  |
  +------------+-----------------------------------------------+

  WE FOUND: batch=1 breaks BatchNorm inside YOLO.
  BatchNorm is a layer that normalises using the MEAN and STD of the batch.
  With only 1 image, mean=that image, std=0. The layer divides by zero.
  So we always kept batch >= 4.

  12.9  WHAT IS AUGMENTATION?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Augmentation = showing the model the same image in many different forms
  so it learns the PATTERN, not the exact image.

  Think of it like learning to recognise a dog:
  You see a dog sitting, standing, running, from left, from right.
  Now you can recognise ANY dog, not just that one photo.

  +---------------------------+------------------------------------------+
  | Augmentation              | What it does (simple)                    |
  +---------------------------+------------------------------------------+
  | Horizontal flip (50%)     | Mirror image left-right. Left breast     |
  |                           | becomes right breast.                    |
  | Brightness change         | Make image brighter or darker.           |
  |                           | Same lesion, different lighting.         |
  | Zoom/Scale (50%)          | Make lesion bigger or smaller randomly.  |
  | Translate (10%)           | Shift image slightly up/down/left/right. |
  | Mosaic (MASS + UNIVERSAL) | Combine 4 different images into 1 tile.  |
  |                           | Model sees more variety per step.        |
  | Mosaic (CALC)             | TURNED OFF. Made calc worse.             |
  | CLAHE                     | Contrast enhancement. TURNED OFF.        |
  |                           | We tested it - it HURT calc detection.   |
  | Mixup                     | OFF. Blend two images together.          |
  +---------------------------+------------------------------------------+

  WHAT WE CHANGED FROM DEFAULT:
    Mosaic = OFF for calc expert (default is ON) - found it hurts
    CLAHE  = OFF always (default is OFF, we confirmed to keep it off)
    Everything else = YOLO defaults

  12.10  WHAT IS IMAGENET PRE-TRAINING?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  We did NOT train YOLO from scratch (random weights from zero).
  We started from weights already trained on IMAGENET.

  WHAT IS IMAGENET?
  A huge dataset of 14 million everyday photos:
  cats, dogs, cars, furniture, food, people, etc.

  WHY DOES THIS HELP US?
  The model already knows how to detect:
    - Edges and corners
    - Textures (smooth vs rough)
    - Shapes (round, elongated, irregular)
    - Contrast differences

  These are EXACTLY what we need for detecting lesions in mammograms.
  We just fine-tune it to recognise mammogram-specific patterns.

  WITHOUT PRE-TRAINING:
    Model starts knowing nothing
    Needs huge data + huge time to learn basic features
    604 mass images would not be enough

  WITH IMAGENET PRE-TRAINING:
    Model already knows shapes and textures
    Just needs to learn "these shapes in this context = cancer"
    604 images is enough to fine-tune

  Load ImageNet pre-trained YOLO11l weights
    |
    v
  For each epoch (89 to 150 times):
    |

    |

    |     |
    |     +-- Apply augmentation (flip, brightness, zoom, mosaic)
    |     |
    |     +-- Model predicts bounding boxes
    |     |
    |     +-- Calculate Loss:
    |     |     Total = 7.5 x (box wrong) + 0.5 x (class wrong) + 1.5 x (edge wrong)
    |     |
    |     +-- SGD updates weights:
    |           new_weight = old_weight

    |                        + 0.937 x old_direction  (momentum)
    |                        - 0.0005 x old_weight    (weight decay)
    |

    |
    +-- Evaluate on val set -- is this the best epoch so far?
    |     YES --> save best.pt
    |     NO  --> continue
    |
    +-- If val loss not improving for 50 epochs (patience) --> STOP early
    |
    v
  SAVE best.pt (highest val mAP50 epoch)
  END

13. WHAT IS A SEED AND WHY 42, 1, 123?
================================================================================

  13.1  WHAT IS A SEED?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Your computer cannot generate truly random numbers.
  It uses a MATHEMATICAL FORMULA that produces numbers that LOOK random.
  But that formula needs a STARTING NUMBER to begin.
  That starting number = SEED.

  Same seed  --> Same sequence of "random" numbers every single time.
  Different seed --> Completely different sequence.

  SIMPLE EXAMPLE:

    random.seed(42)
    random.randint(1,100)  --> always gives 82
    random.randint(1,100)  --> always gives 15

    random.seed(42)        --> reset
    random.randint(1,100)  --> gives 82 again (SAME!)

    random.seed(1)         --> different seed
    random.randint(1,100)  --> gives 18 (DIFFERENT!)

  13.2  HOW SEED WORKS IN OUR PROJECT
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  When we split patients into train/val/test, we shuffle the patient list
  randomly first, then split. The seed controls HOW it shuffles.

  All patients = [P001, P002, P003, P004, P005, P006, P007, P008, P009, P010]

  seed=42  --> shuffle --> [P007, P002, P009, P001, P005, P003, P008, P010, P004, P006]
               split   --> Train: P007,P002,P009,P001,P005,P003
                           Val  : P008,P010
                           Test : P004,P006

  seed=1   --> shuffle --> [P003, P008, P001, P006, P010, P007, P004, P002, P005, P009]
               split   --> Train: P003,P008,P001,P006,P010,P007
                           Val  : P004,P002
                           Test : P005,P009

  seed=123 --> shuffle --> [P010, P005, P004, P008, P001, P009, P006, P003, P007, P002]
               split   --> Train: P010,P005,P004,P008,P001,P009
                           Val  : P006,P003
                           Test : P007,P002

  Each seed = COMPLETELY DIFFERENT patients in train/test.
  Each seed = A different experiment.

  13.3  WHY DO WE USE 3 SEEDS?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Imagine you trained only with seed=42 and got mAP50 = 0.65.

  Is that a good model -- OR did you just get lucky?
  Maybe seed=42 accidentally put all easy cases in the test set.
  Maybe all hard cases went to training. You do not know.

  With 3 seeds:

    seed=42  --> mAP50 = 0.516
    seed=1   --> mAP50 = 0.436
    seed=123 --> mAP50 = 0.544
    Mean = 0.499 +/- 0.046

  Now you can say: "Across 3 different splits, our model consistently
  gets ~0.50 mAP50." That is a trustworthy claim.

  13.4  WHY SPECIFICALLY 42, 1, AND 123?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  HONEST ANSWER: There is nothing special about these numbers.

  +--------+--------------------------------------------------------------+
  | Seed   | Why chosen                                                   |
  +--------+--------------------------------------------------------------+
  | 42     | Famous in computer science. From "The Hitchhiker's Guide to  |
  |        | the Galaxy" - 42 is "the answer to everything." Almost every |
  |        | ML paper uses 42 as their first seed. It became the default. |
  | 1      | Simplest possible seed. Very different from 42 so gives a   |
  |        | genuinely different shuffle.                                  |
  | 123    | Easy to remember. Common second choice in papers. Very       |
  |        | different from both 42 and 1.                                |
  +--------+--------------------------------------------------------------+

  We could have used 7, 99, 500 -- the results would be equally valid.
  The ONLY requirements are:

    1. Seeds must be DIFFERENT from each other (different splits)
    2. Seeds must be FIXED BEFORE the experiment (no cherry-picking)
    3. Seeds must be REPORTED in the paper (for reproducibility)

  13.5  WHAT WOULD HAPPEN WITH DIFFERENT SEEDS?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  If you used seeds 200, 300, 400 instead:

    seed=200 --> mAP50 might be 0.48
    seed=300 --> mAP50 might be 0.52
    seed=400 --> mAP50 might be 0.45
    Mean = 0.483 +/- 0.035

  Slightly different numbers, but the STORY stays the same.
  The model consistently gets ~0.48-0.52. That is what matters.

  13.6  WHY NOT USE 10 OR 20 SEEDS?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  More seeds = more trustworthy results. BUT:

  3 seeds  = 12 training runs  (~2 days on RTX 5090)
  10 seeds = 40 training runs  (~7 days)
  20 seeds = 80 training runs  (~14 days)

  For a conference paper (AAAI, MIDL), n=3 seeds is acceptable and common.
  For a journal paper, n=5 seeds would be better.

  We state in the paper: "n=3 is a pilot evaluation.
  Formal significance testing deferred to journal version."

15. FROC vs mAP50 - WHICH ONE MATTERS MORE?
================================================================================

  SHORT ANSWER: FROC IS PRIMARY. ALWAYS. NO DEBATE.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

  15.1  WHAT EACH METRIC ANSWERS
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

  FROC S@2 answers:
    "At 2 false alarms per scan, how many real cancers does AI catch?"
    --> This is what DOCTORS care about.
    --> This is what PATIENTS care about.
    --> This is what CLINICAL DEPLOYMENT cares about.

  mAP50 answers:
    "How well does the AI draw tight boxes around objects?"
    --> This is what COMPUTER VISION researchers care about.
    --> No radiologist has ever used mAP50 to judge a clinical system.

  15.2  SIMPLE ANALOGY
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Imagine a security guard checking bags at an airport.

  +----------+----------------------------+---------------------------------+
  | Metric   | What it measures           | Airport equivalent              |
  +----------+----------------------------+---------------------------------+
  | FROC S@2 | Of all real threats, how   | Did you catch the real bombs    |
  |          | many caught at 2 false     | without too many false alarms?  |
  |          | alarms per hour?           |                                 |
  | mAP50    | How precisely did you      | Did you point to the exact      |
  |          | identify exactly which     | compartment in the bag?         |
  |          | pocket the threat was in?  |                                 |
  +----------+----------------------------+---------------------------------+

  The airport cares about CATCHING THREATS.
  Not which exact pocket the threat was in.
